# Data Analytics Interview Preparation

## 1. Tell me about your data analytics background.
I am building my data analytics skills through the Power Learn Project, where I am learning Python, data cleaning, exploratory data analysis, SQL and data visualisation. My background also includes data annotation with Atlas Capture and IT support at Human Practice Foundation. These experiences strengthened my attention to detail, troubleshooting and ability to follow structured processes. I recently built a retail sales analysis capstone using synthetic data to practise turning raw records into useful business insights.

## 2. How do you approach cleaning a dataset?
I first understand the business question and inspect the dataset's shape, columns, data types and sample records. I check completeness, duplicates, validity, consistency, unusual values and date coverage. I then apply appropriate corrections, such as standardising text, converting data types and handling missing values based on context. I keep a cleaning log, validate the results and preserve the raw source data so the work is reproducible.

## 3. What is exploratory data analysis (EDA), and why is it useful?
EDA is the process of summarising and visualising data before drawing conclusions. I use descriptive statistics and charts to understand distributions, spot outliers, compare groups and explore relationships. It helps identify data issues and patterns that guide deeper analysis. In my Duka retail capstone, I compared sales across branches, categories, products and months.

## 4. What is the difference between an INNER JOIN and a LEFT JOIN in SQL?
An INNER JOIN returns only rows with matching keys in both tables. A LEFT JOIN keeps every row from the left table and adds matching information from the right table; if there is no match, right-side columns are NULL. I would use an INNER JOIN when I only need matched records, and a LEFT JOIN when I need to retain all records from the main table.

## 5. Which KPIs would you use to monitor retail sales performance?
I would start with net revenue, transaction count, average transaction value, units sold, discount rate and repeat-customer activity. I would define each KPI clearly and calculate it consistently over a stated period. I would break results down by branch, product, category and month to understand differences. A dashboard helps managers monitor these measures, but a trend alone does not prove what caused a change.

# STAR Interview Examples

Use these as preparation guides and keep every detail accurate to your own experience.

## STAR 1 — IT support at Human Practice Foundation

**Situation:** During my IT attachment at Human Practice Foundation, staff needed support with day-to-day technology and digital tools.

**Task:** I was expected to help users resolve technical issues and support reliable use of equipment and systems.

**Action:** I listened to the user's description, checked the symptoms, worked through basic hardware or software troubleshooting and explained steps in simple language. Where necessary, I followed the organisation's support process and sought assistance for issues beyond my level.

**Result:** I helped users get back to their work and developed stronger troubleshooting, communication and problem-solving skills. Add one specific real example you personally handled when answering an interview question.

## STAR 2 — Quality in data annotation

**Situation:** In my freelance data annotation work with Atlas Capture, datasets needed to be labelled and structured consistently for AI/ML workflows.

**Task:** My responsibility was to follow annotation guidelines and produce accurate labels while checking my work.

**Action:** I reviewed instructions before starting, labelled records according to the defined categories, checked uncertain items against the guidelines and reviewed completed work for consistency and errors.

**Result:** This helped me build careful working habits, attention to detail and an understanding of why reliable labelled data matters to AI/ML projects. Add a specific verified example or outcome if you have one; do not invent a percentage or volume.
